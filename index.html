01 — Origin

1980

Production begins in Kragujevac

A small car from a city of engineers

The Yugo was built by Zavodi Crvena Zastava in Kragujevac, a city in central Serbia whose works had been casting cannon since the 1850s. By the time of the Yugo, Zastava had been building Fiat designs under licence for more than two decades.

The new car took the mechanical basis of the Fiat 127 and gave it a squarer, more upright three-door body. The 55, named for its output in horsepower, became the version most people outside Yugoslavia would come to know.

03 — The Story

Across the Atlantic, and home again

In 1985 the Yugo arrived in the United States at $3,990, the least expensive new car on sale there. It was simple, light and easy to work on, and for many buyers it was the first new car they had ever owned.

Abroad, its reputation was hard-won and often unkind, and the jokes outlived its years on the market. At home it was the car of families, students, taxi ranks and first jobs, driven for decades and kept running with patience and care.

04 — Legacy

From Ljubljana to Skopje, the Yugo, known at home as the Koral, remains part of family memory: the first car, the car for the coast in August, the car kept going long after newer ones had come and gone. It is remembered less as a product than as a shared piece of everyday life in a country that no longer exists.
